Delivering Net Zero: Lessons From Our Latest Development

With the Future Homes Standard approaching and planning authorities demanding more, net zero has become a commercial imperative. Here's how W13 Group is delivering sustainable homes across the West Midlands.

Beyond the Buzzword

Sustainability in construction has moved far beyond a marketing exercise. With the Future Homes Standard set to come into full effect, and local planning authorities increasingly requiring demonstrable environmental commitments, net zero is now a practical, commercial imperative for every developer.

At W13 Group, we've been integrating sustainable practices across our developments for years. Here, we share the lessons and approaches that have proved most effective.

Fabric First: The Foundation

The most cost-effective route to low-carbon homes starts with the building envelope. Our approach prioritises:

  • Enhanced insulation standards: Exceeding Part L requirements with U-values typically 15-20% better than minimum compliance
  • Thermal bridging reduction: Detailed junction design to minimise heat loss at critical points
  • Airtightness: Targeting results well below the regulatory maximum, reducing uncontrolled ventilation losses
  • High-performance glazing: Triple glazing in exposed elevations, with solar control where appropriate

Getting the fabric right reduces the demand for energy in the first place — making the building inherently more efficient regardless of which heating or ventilation system is installed.

Low-Carbon Energy Systems

Air source heat pumps

Our recent developments have transitioned from gas boilers to air source heat pumps (ASHPs) as the primary heating source. Combined with underfloor heating systems — which operate at lower flow temperatures — ASHPs deliver comfortable, efficient heating with significantly reduced carbon emissions.

Solar PV integration

Roof-mounted photovoltaic panels are now standard across our residential schemes. Typical installations generate 2.5-4kWp per dwelling, covering a substantial proportion of household electricity demand and reducing grid dependency.

Battery storage and smart controls

Where feasible, we're incorporating battery storage to maximise self-consumption of generated solar energy. Smart heating controls and home energy management systems give residents visibility and control over their energy use.

Embodied Carbon: The Hidden Challenge

Operational energy is only part of the picture. The carbon embedded in materials — from manufacturing, transport, and construction — can account for up to 50% of a building's whole-life carbon impact.

Our strategies for reducing embodied carbon include:

  • Specifying lower-carbon concrete mixes where structural requirements allow
  • Using timber frame construction for appropriate building types
  • Sourcing materials locally where possible to reduce transport emissions
  • Designing for longevity and adaptability to extend building lifespan
  • Targeting 100% diversion of construction waste from landfill

"Net zero isn't a destination — it's a direction. Every project we deliver needs to be measurably better than the last. The technology and supply chain are evolving rapidly, and we're committed to evolving with them."

Biodiversity and Ecology

The Environment Act 2021 requires a minimum 10% Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG) on all major developments. Our approach goes beyond minimum compliance, incorporating green roofs, native planting, wildlife corridors, and sustainable drainage systems (SuDS) that enhance the ecological value of our sites.

The Commercial Case

Sustainable homes aren't just better for the environment — they're better for business. Energy-efficient homes command premium values, attract ESG-conscious investors in the BTR sector, and face fewer obstacles through the planning process. They also future-proof our portfolio against tightening regulations.

As EPC requirements become more stringent and mortgage lenders begin differentiating rates based on energy performance, the commercial advantage of sustainable construction will only grow.

What's Next

We're currently exploring modular and modern methods of construction (MMC) to further reduce waste and improve build quality. We're also investigating whole-life carbon assessment tools to better measure and reduce the total environmental impact of our developments.
